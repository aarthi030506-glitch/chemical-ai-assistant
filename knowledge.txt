# CHEMICAL ENGINEERING KNOWLEDGE BASE

## 1. DISTILLATION

Distillation is a separation process used to separate components of a liquid mixture based on differences in volatility or boiling points. In a distillation column, vapor and liquid phases come into contact repeatedly, allowing the more volatile component to become concentrated in the vapor phase.

The reboiler supplies heat to generate vapor. The condenser removes heat from the overhead vapor and converts it into liquid. The distillate is the overhead product, while the bottoms product leaves from the bottom of the column.

Reflux is the portion of condensed overhead liquid that is returned to the distillation column. Increasing reflux generally improves separation but also increases energy consumption.

The reflux ratio is the ratio of liquid returned to the column as reflux to the liquid withdrawn as distillate.

The relative volatility between components is an important factor in distillation. Higher relative volatility generally makes separation easier, while low relative volatility makes separation more difficult.

Common distillation equipment includes trays, packing, reboilers, condensers, reflux drums, and distillation columns.

## 2. HEAT TRANSFER

Heat transfer is the movement of thermal energy from a region of higher temperature to a region of lower temperature.

The three basic modes of heat transfer are conduction, convection, and radiation.

Conduction is heat transfer through a stationary material due to a temperature gradient.

Convection is heat transfer between a surface and a moving fluid. It can be natural convection or forced convection.

Radiation is heat transfer through electromagnetic waves and does not require a material medium.

For sensible heating or cooling:

Q = m Cp Delta T

where Q is heat transferred, m is mass flow rate or mass depending on the application, Cp is specific heat capacity, and Delta T is temperature change.

A heat exchanger is equipment used to transfer heat between two fluids without necessarily mixing them.

In a shell-and-tube heat exchanger, one fluid flows through tubes while another fluid flows around the tubes inside the shell.

The overall heat-transfer relationship can be represented as:

Q = U A Delta Tlm

where U is the overall heat-transfer coefficient, A is heat-transfer area, and Delta Tlm is the logarithmic mean temperature difference.

## 3. REYNOLDS NUMBER

Reynolds number is a dimensionless quantity used to characterize fluid flow. It helps determine whether flow is likely to be laminar or turbulent.

For flow inside a circular pipe:

Re = rho v D / mu

where rho is fluid density, v is average velocity, D is pipe diameter, and mu is dynamic viscosity.

For many internal pipe-flow situations, low Reynolds numbers indicate laminar flow while sufficiently high Reynolds numbers indicate turbulent flow.

For flow in a circular pipe, Reynolds number below approximately 2300 is commonly associated with laminar flow, while values above approximately 4000 are commonly associated with turbulent flow. The region between these values is often called the transition region.

---

## 4. IDEAL GAS LAW

The ideal gas law relates pressure, volume, temperature, and amount of gas:

PV = nRT

where P is pressure, V is volume, n is number of moles, R is the gas constant, and T is absolute temperature.

Temperature must be expressed in an absolute temperature scale when using the ideal gas law.

The ideal gas equation can be rearranged to calculate pressure, volume, temperature, or number of moles when the other variables are known.

Real gases can deviate from ideal-gas behavior, especially at high pressures and low temperatures.

---

## 5. FLUID MECHANICS

Fluid mechanics is the study of fluids at rest and in motion.

Fluid properties important in Chemical Engineering include density, viscosity, specific gravity, vapor pressure, and surface tension.

Pressure is force per unit area.

For an incompressible fluid, the continuity equation can be expressed as:

A1 v1 = A2 v2

where A is cross-sectional area and v is average velocity.

Bernoulli's equation relates pressure, velocity, elevation, and energy losses in fluid flow.

The pressure drop in a pipe depends on factors such as fluid viscosity, density, velocity, pipe diameter, pipe length, and pipe roughness.

Pumps are used to increase the pressure or energy of liquids, while compressors are commonly used for gases.

---

## 6. MASS TRANSFER

Mass transfer is the movement of a chemical species from one location to another due to a concentration difference or other driving force.

Diffusion is the molecular movement of a species caused by a concentration gradient.

Fick's law describes molecular diffusion. For one-dimensional diffusion:

J = -D dC/dz

where J is diffusive flux, D is the diffusion coefficient, C is concentration, and z is distance.

Common mass-transfer operations include absorption, stripping, humidification, drying, extraction, adsorption, and membrane separation.

Mass-transfer equipment can include packed columns, tray columns, spray towers, and membrane modules.

---

## 7. ABSORPTION

Absorption is a mass-transfer operation in which one or more components of a gas mixture are transferred into a liquid solvent.

Gas absorption is commonly used for removing unwanted components from gas streams.

In a packed absorption column, gas and liquid flow through packing material that provides a large contact area.

The driving force for absorption is related to the difference between the actual concentration and the equilibrium concentration.

Absorption and stripping are related operations. Absorption transfers a component from gas to liquid, while stripping transfers a component from liquid to gas.

---

## 8. LIQUID-LIQUID EXTRACTION

Liquid-liquid extraction is a separation process in which a solute is transferred from one liquid phase to another immiscible or partially miscible liquid phase.

The solvent should have a high affinity for the desired solute and should preferably have low affinity for the other components.

Extraction is useful when distillation is difficult because of close boiling points or thermal sensitivity.

Important concepts include distribution coefficient, selectivity, equilibrium stages, solvent-to-feed ratio, and phase equilibrium.

---

## 9. ADSORPTION

Adsorption is a surface phenomenon in which molecules from a gas or liquid phase accumulate on the surface of a solid adsorbent.

Common adsorbents include activated carbon, silica gel, alumina, and molecular sieves.

Adsorption can be used for purification, removal of contaminants, drying, and separation of components.

The two major types are physical adsorption and chemisorption.

Physical adsorption generally involves weaker intermolecular forces, while chemisorption involves stronger chemical interactions.

---

## 10. MEMBRANE SEPARATION

Membrane separation uses a selective membrane to separate components of a mixture.

Separation depends on differences in properties such as molecular size, diffusivity, solubility, and permeability.

Common membrane processes include reverse osmosis, ultrafiltration, microfiltration, and gas separation.

Reverse osmosis is commonly used for water purification and desalination.

Membrane processes can operate with lower energy requirements than some conventional thermal separation processes because phase change may not be required.

---

## 11. DRYING

Drying is the removal of moisture or another volatile component from a solid material by evaporation.

Drying commonly involves simultaneous heat and mass transfer.

The drying rate depends on factors such as temperature, humidity, air velocity, surface area, moisture content, and properties of the material.

Common industrial dryers include tray dryers, rotary dryers, spray dryers, fluidized-bed dryers, and drum dryers.

---

## 12. EVAPORATION

Evaporation is the concentration of a solution by vaporizing part of the solvent.

Evaporators are commonly used in chemical, food, pharmaceutical, and wastewater-treatment industries.

The main objective is usually to remove solvent while retaining the nonvolatile solute.

Multiple-effect evaporation can reduce steam consumption by using vapor generated in one effect as the heating medium for another effect.

---

## 13. FILTRATION

Filtration is a mechanical separation process used to separate solid particles from a fluid by passing the mixture through a porous medium.

The solid retained by the filter is called the filter cake.

Common filtration equipment includes plate-and-frame filters, rotary drum filters, cartridge filters, and vacuum filters.

Filtration performance depends on pressure difference, filter medium resistance, cake resistance, viscosity, particle size, and solids concentration.

---

## 14. MECHANICAL OPERATIONS

Mechanical operations involve the physical handling and separation of solid particles and solid-fluid systems.

Common mechanical operations include size reduction, screening, filtration, sedimentation, centrifugation, mixing, and conveying.

Size reduction equipment includes crushers, grinders, ball mills, and pulverizers.

Screening separates solid particles based on particle size.

Sedimentation separates particles from a fluid under the influence of gravity.

Centrifugation uses centrifugal force to enhance separation.

---

## 15. THERMODYNAMICS

Chemical Engineering thermodynamics deals with energy, heat, work, equilibrium, and the properties of chemical systems.

The first law of thermodynamics is an energy conservation principle.

For a closed system, the change in internal energy is related to heat supplied and work done.

Enthalpy is defined as:

H = U + PV

where H is enthalpy, U is internal energy, P is pressure, and V is volume.

Entropy is a thermodynamic property associated with energy dispersal and irreversibility.

Chemical Engineering thermodynamics is used in phase equilibrium, energy balances, refrigeration, power cycles, and separation processes.

---

## 16. MATERIAL BALANCE

A material balance is based on the conservation of mass.

The general material balance can be expressed as:

Input + Generation - Output - Consumption = Accumulation

For a steady-state system without reaction:

Input = Output

Material balances are used in chemical process design, plant operation, process analysis, and equipment sizing.

Material balances can be performed around individual units or an entire process.

---

## 17. ENERGY BALANCE

An energy balance is based on the conservation of energy.

For a general process:

Energy in - Energy out + Energy generated - Energy consumed = Energy accumulated

For many steady-state processes, accumulation is zero.

Energy balances are used to determine heating requirements, cooling requirements, work requirements, and energy efficiency.

Heat duty is the amount of heat that must be supplied to or removed from a process.

---

## 18. CHEMICAL REACTION ENGINEERING

Chemical Reaction Engineering deals with the analysis and design of chemical reactors.

A reactor is equipment in which chemical reactions are carried out under controlled conditions.

Common reactor types include batch reactors, continuous stirred-tank reactors (CSTRs), and plug-flow reactors (PFRs).

Reaction rate describes how quickly reactants are consumed or products are formed.

Factors affecting reaction rate include temperature, concentration, pressure, catalyst, and reaction mechanism.

Catalysts increase reaction rate by providing an alternative reaction pathway with lower activation energy.

---

## 19. PROCESS CONTROL

Process control is the use of measurement, control strategies, and manipulated variables to maintain process conditions near desired values.

A typical feedback control loop contains a sensor, transmitter, controller, final control element, and process.

Important process variables include temperature, pressure, flow rate, and liquid level.

A controller compares the measured process variable with the set point and generates a control action.

PID control consists of proportional, integral, and derivative actions.

Process control improves safety, product quality, stability, and operating efficiency.

---

## 20. PROCESS INSTRUMENTATION

Instrumentation involves the measurement and monitoring of process variables.

Common process sensors measure temperature, pressure, flow, level, composition, and other physical properties.

RTD sensors are commonly used for temperature measurement and operate based on the change in electrical resistance with temperature.

Pressure measurement devices include manometers, Bourdon gauges, diaphragm gauges, and pressure transmitters.

Flow measurement devices include orifice meters, venturi meters, rotameters, and magnetic flow meters.

---

## 21. PROCESS EQUIPMENT

Chemical Engineering equipment includes reactors, heat exchangers, distillation columns, absorbers, pumps, compressors, filters, dryers, evaporators, and storage tanks.

Equipment selection depends on process requirements, operating conditions, material properties, safety, cost, and maintenance requirements.

Heat exchangers transfer thermal energy between process streams.

Reactors provide controlled environments for chemical reactions.

Distillation columns provide vapor-liquid contact for separation.

---

## 22. PETROLEUM REFINING

Petroleum refining converts crude oil into useful products such as gasoline, diesel, kerosene, LPG, lubricants, and petrochemical feedstocks.

Crude oil is separated into fractions based on differences in boiling points using atmospheric and vacuum distillation.

Important refinery processes include catalytic cracking, hydrocracking, catalytic reforming, alkylation, hydrotreating, and desulfurization.

Catalytic cracking converts heavier hydrocarbons into lighter, more valuable products.

Hydrotreating uses hydrogen and catalysts to remove impurities such as sulfur and nitrogen.

---

## 23. INDUSTRIAL SAFETY

Chemical process safety focuses on preventing fires, explosions, toxic releases, equipment failures, and other major accidents.

Important safety practices include hazard identification, risk assessment, proper process design, pressure relief, emergency shutdown systems, personal protective equipment, and safe operating procedures.

Chemical plants must consider hazards associated with toxic, flammable, corrosive, reactive, and high-pressure materials.

Process safety is important during normal operation, startup, shutdown, maintenance, and emergency conditions.

---

## 24. DIMENSIONLESS NUMBERS IN CHEMICAL ENGINEERING

Dimensionless numbers are widely used to analyze transport phenomena and process equipment.

Reynolds number compares inertial forces with viscous forces.

Prandtl number relates momentum diffusivity to thermal diffusivity.

Schmidt number relates momentum diffusivity to mass diffusivity.

Nusselt number relates convective heat transfer to conductive heat transfer.

Sherwood number relates convective mass transfer to molecular diffusion.

Dimensionless correlations are frequently used in heat-transfer and mass-transfer equipment design.

---

## 25. COMMON CHEMICAL ENGINEERING CALCULATIONS

Important calculations in Chemical Engineering include:

* Reynolds number
* Heat duty
* Material balance
* Energy balance
* Ideal gas calculations
* Pressure drop
* Heat exchanger calculations
* Mass-transfer calculations
* Reaction-rate calculations
* Pump and compressor calculations

Engineering calculations should use consistent units and appropriate physical-property values.

For numerical problems, the required variables should be identified first, the appropriate equation selected, values substituted with consistent units, and the final result checked for physical reasonableness.
